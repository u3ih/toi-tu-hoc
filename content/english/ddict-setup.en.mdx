---
title: Setting up Ddict
description: The lookup tool I use daily — double-click a word, get the meaning, never leave the page.
---

Ddict is a browser extension. It lets you look words up **right on the page you are reading** — no
new tab, no copying and pasting.

Of every tool I have used, this made the biggest difference to my reading.

<Callout type="story">
I used to read with a dictionary tab open beside me. Every unfamiliar word meant: select, switch tab,
paste, read, switch back, find my place again.

That is only ten seconds. But thirty times in an evening and I had lost the thread of the story
entirely — so I put the book down.

This tool taught me no vocabulary at all. It only removed that friction. And my reading volume went
up several times over.
</Callout>

## Installation

<Steps>
### 1. Install the extension
Search for **"Ddict"** on the Chrome Web Store and install it like any other extension. Ddict also
runs on Edge and other Chromium browsers.

### 2. Pin it to the toolbar
Click the puzzle-piece icon in the top right of the browser and pin Ddict. You will be opening it
often.

### 3. Choose the target language
Open Ddict's settings and set the translation language to **Vietnamese** if you are starting out.

Later, once you are steadier, switch it to **English** so you read definitions in English itself. I
go into why in [Monolingual dictionaries](/english/monolingual-dictionary/).

### 4. Choose how lookups are triggered
Ddict offers a few ways to bring the popup up: **double-click** a word, **select** a phrase, or
**right-click** and pick from the menu.

I use double-click for single words and selection for whole sentences. Try all three and keep
whichever feels least intrusive.

### 5. Turn on read-aloud
There is a pronunciation option in the settings. I keep it on, because hearing the word at the moment
I look it up sticks far better than reading a phonetic transcription.
</Steps>

<Callout type="info" title="Menu names may differ slightly">
Ddict updates fairly often, so menu labels can change between versions. If you don't see exactly what
I described, look for anything containing *Language*, *Translate* or *Speech* — the meaning is the
same.
</Callout>

## Checking that it works

Open any English page and double-click a word. If a small box appears with a definition and a
pronunciation button, you are done.

<Callout type="warning" title="Nothing appears?">
Three causes I run into:

- The extension has no permission on that site. Click the Ddict icon and allow it to run there.
- The page renders text as images, which some online book readers do. The extension cannot read text
  inside a picture.
- The trigger you configured is not the one you are trying. Reopen the settings and check whether
  double-click or selection is enabled.
</Callout>

## What else Ddict does

A few things I use on top:

- **Translate the whole page** when I only need the gist, fast.
- **Translate PDFs** — handy for documents.
- **Synonyms and example sentences** right inside the popup. Example sentences are what I most often
  copy into Anki.

Ddict is usable for free, with some paid features. The free version is enough for my daily reading.

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't lean on whole-page translation">
There was a phase where I translated whole pages and read the Vietnamese to go faster. I understood
everything, and my English did not improve at all — because my eyes never touched the English.

Now I only translate a whole page when I need information urgently, not when I am studying.
</Callout>
