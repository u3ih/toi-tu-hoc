The goal of the first month is **not** to get better. After 30 days I still understood very little.

The goal is to build the habit and the toolkit, so the following 11 months run without needing
thought.

## Week 1 — Build the system

| Day | What I did |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Install Anki and [Ddict](/english/ddict-setup/) |
| 2 | Pick **one** listening source and **one** reading source, then stop searching |
| 3–5 | Every day: 20 minutes active listening, 10 minutes reading |
| 6 | Start reviewing a ~2000-word foundation vocabulary deck |
| 7 | Sit down and ask: what made me want to quit? Fix that specific thing. |

<Callout type="tip" title="Pick material you actually enjoy">
I once picked a very "worthwhile" economics podcast. Three days in I was bored and dropped the whole
study habit with it.

The next time I picked something I genuinely wanted to watch. Less "worthwhile", but I stuck with it
for months.

You will quit from boredom long before you quit from difficulty.
</Callout>

## Week 2 — Increase listening volume

I pushed active listening up to 30–40 minutes, and started background listening in spare moments.

Two things I took away from this week:

- **Relistening to the same material 2–3 times** beats constantly hunting for something new. The
  second pass always catches what the first one lost.
- **No Vietnamese subtitles.** I tried it and found my eyes were only reading the subtitles while my
  ears did almost nothing. English subtitles are fine.

## Week 3 — Start making my own cards

This is where I started creating Anki cards from sentences I met while listening and reading. The
specifics are in [Vocabulary & Anki](/english/vocabulary-anki/).

<Callout type="warning" title="Ten cards a day, maximum">
That week I was enthusiastic — some days I made more than 40 cards. It felt very productive.

Three weeks later I opened Anki to over 400 cards due. I closed it and did not open it again for a
month.

Since then I have kept a hard limit of 10 cards a day.
</Callout>

## Week 4 — Change nothing

This week I deliberately added nothing new. No new app, no new source, no new technique.

The only job of week 4 is to prove that the schedule I set is a schedule I can keep.

At the end of the week I answered three questions:

1. On my busiest day, how many minutes did I still manage?
2. Which task did I avoid the most? Why?
3. Which material do I genuinely want to go back to?

<Callout type="story">
My answer to question 3 back then was a long-running animated series. It sounded childish and I was
a little embarrassed.

But that is the one I watched more than 200 episodes of over the following year. It was my main
listening source for that entire year, and I learned more from it than from every textbook combined.

Don't be embarrassed if the thing you like doesn't look serious.
</Callout>
