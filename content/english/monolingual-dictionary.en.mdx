title: Monolingual dictionaries
description: When I dropped Vietnamese definitions, and how to switch without the shock.
---

A monolingual dictionary explains English words **in English**.

Switching to one was one of my biggest jumps. It was also the step I put off the longest, because it
scared me.

## Why switch

**A one-word translation almost always loses the nuance.** Vietnamese has *nhìn*, *ngắm*, *liếc*,
*trừng*. In English those become several different words. It works the same way in reverse — and a
bilingual dictionary usually gives you exactly one.

**Reading an English definition is itself a small reading exercise.** A few dozen lookups a day is a
few dozen reps, entirely free.

**You gradually stop translating in your head.** This is the one that matters most. As long as you
route through your own language, you are slower than real conversation.

## When you are ready to switch

<Callout type="tip" title="The 30-second test">
Look up a word you already know well, but read its English definition.

If you understand the definition without having to look up anything else, you are ready.
</Callout>

For me that landed somewhere around a vocabulary of 3000–5000 words.

I tried switching earlier and failed: I would look up one word, find three unknown words in the
definition, look those up and find six more. Demoralising.

## Switch gradually, not all at once

This is the least painful path I found.

In [Ddict](/english/ddict-setup/), instead of flipping the target language to English straight away,
I did this for about a month:

<Steps>
### 1. Read the English part first
On every lookup, force yourself to read the English definition and the example sentence first.

### 2. Only fall back to the translation when genuinely stuck
Treat it as a safety net, not the first place you look.

### 3. After a few weeks, switch the target language to English for good
By then you will notice you barely touch the translation any more.
</Steps>

<Callout type="story">
In my first week on a monolingual dictionary, everything visibly slowed down and I nearly went back.

I promised myself two more weeks. Around day ten, I looked up a word, read the English definition,
understood it immediately — and realised I had not translated that definition into Vietnamese at all.

That was the first time I caught myself *thinking* in English, even if only for one sentence.
</Callout>

## Which kind of dictionary

| Kind | What it is | When to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learner's dictionary | Definitions written with a limited ~2000-word vocabulary | Start here |
| Full native dictionary | More precise and detailed, but harder definitions | When the above stops being enough |
| Collocation dictionary | Shows which words habitually go together | Very useful once you start writing |
| Frequency dictionary | No definitions, just how common a word is | To decide which words are worth a card |

I started with a learner's dictionary and only moved to a full native one after about a year, when
the simplified definitions stopped saying enough.
